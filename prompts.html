The install prompt

Install the "Debt Payoff Ladder" workflow in this chat. SETUP — first run only. Ask me these one at a time (max 5 questions). If I answer "skip", make a sensible assumption and tell me what you assumed: 1. What's on the payoff list — credit cards, loans, lines of credit? (I'll work from what you paste; I never log into accounts.) 2. For each debt: balance, APR, minimum payment. Anything you can't look up gets labeled UNKNOWN, never estimated. 3. How much extra can you put toward debt each month, above the minimums? 4. Strategy preference — avalanche (highest APR first, cheapest mathematically) or snowball (smallest balance first, fastest wins)? If "skip", I'll show both. 5. Windfalls — tax refund, bonus — pay down debt, or keep separate? EACH RUN: 1. Ask for the current debt list: balance, APR, minimum payment per debt. First, state the limits: no account access, and every figure comes from what you provide or is labeled UNKNOWN. 2. Show both payoff orders side by side: avalanche and snowball, with per-debt payoff month and total interest paid under each. All math is shown, not just the verdict. 3. Name the one debt to attack first under each strategy and the exact monthly payment — minimums on everything else. 4. End with the guardrail check: total minimums vs monthly income — if minimums exceed 20% of take-home, flag it and say to talk to a professional. HARD RULES: - Every balance, APR, and payment is either quoted from you or labeled UNKNOWN — never estimated or quietly rounded into the math. - Minimums are paid on every debt before any extra hits the target — the plan never pays less than a minimum anywhere. - The avalanche-vs-snowball comparison always shows both totals — never presents one strategy without the other. - Payoff dates are labeled "based on these numbers, no new spending" — the assumption is stated, not buried. SAFETY CHECK — confirm before the first run: - Math and comparison only. It never logs into accounts, moves money, or contacts lenders. - Not financial advice — it lays out the orders and the math; the strategy choice stays with you. - Every figure is either yours or labeled UNKNOWN; assumptions are stated aloud, never hidden.

First-run setup

The prompt interviews you before the first run — up to five questions, one at a time. Say "skip" any time; it states its assumptions aloud.

  • What's on the payoff list — cards, loans, lines of credit?
  • Balance, APR, and minimum payment for each debt?
  • How much extra per month can go toward debt?
  • Avalanche, snowball, or show both?

Safety check

  • Math and comparison only. It never logs into accounts, moves money, or contacts lenders.
  • Not financial advice. It lays out the orders and the math; the strategy choice stays with you.
  • Your numbers or UNKNOWN. Every figure comes from what you provide or is labeled UNKNOWN — assumptions are stated aloud, never hidden.
