Microsoft Beats, Alphabet Burns Cash, Apple's First Touchscreen Mac

October 29, 2026 · 7:41 · with Alex & Jordan

In this episode

  • **Microsoft beats, Alphabet burns cash as AI spending faces its prove-it moment (earnings reported after close Wed Oct 28; Reuters/CNBC)**: Microsoft revenue $92.3B (+19%), adjusted EPS $4.89 vs $4.73 expected; Azure revenue disclosed in dollars for the first time at $33.2B (+48%, above 44–45% guide); capex $45.2B yet free cash flow +$18.6B (more than double forecast); shares +4% after hours; capex guidance trimmed to ~$175B. Alphabet: Google Cloud +103% to $30.7B, but revenue/EPS/search ads missed; capex $51.4B exceeded operating cash flow, leaving a ~$9.1B free cash flow deficit — its second straight negative quarter and second since its 2004 IPO; shares −10%. Fed held rates at 3.75%–4.00% in a 10–2 vote. Fresh — yesterday's episode covered Microsoft's buyout offer, not these results.
  • **Apple unveils its first touchscreen MacBook Pro at "Pro and Tell" (Wed Oct 28, Los Angeles)**: 14-inch and 16-inch MacBook Pros with tandem-OLED PROLED XDR display (up to 2,000 nits, 10,000,000:1 contrast, up to 120Hz), Dynamic Island, M5 Pro/Max chips, up to 128GB unified memory; 14-inch from $2,899, 16-inch from $3,399, M5 Max versions from $4,499/$4,799 (~$400 premium over non-touch models, which stay on sale); preorders Oct 28, availability Nov 6. Also: iPad mini refresh with OLED display and A20 Pro, from $699. Genuinely new angle vs yesterday's episode, which covered the M6 MacBook Pro/iMac and only previewed the touchscreen Mac as expected.
  • **"Icepick" leak exposes 41,000+ DHS personnel credentials (published Wed Oct 28)**: Hacker collective "Department of Peace" with Distributed Denial of Secrets published login credentials for 41,000+ DHS employees and contractors across seven component agencies (ICE, CBP, CISA among them); data includes emails, phone numbers, organizational details; breach reportedly originated from an Okta single sign-on system (latest dataset update Aug 19); Okta says its systems were not compromised; DHS has not yet commented. Fresh — not covered in prior episodes.
  • **Security quick hits**: PHP2Shell flaws (CVE-2026-109618/109619) — CISA KEV deadline today Oct 29 for federal agencies; unauthenticated RCE in PHP, exploit attempts seen on honeypots. Cisco Secure Firewall flaw (CVE-2026-76418, CVSS 8.6) actively exploited — unauthenticated attackers can force firewall reloads, breaking VPN connectivity. Alpine Linux's GitLab hit by an exploit attempt on a CVSS 10.0 GitLab/Ruby flaw (CVE-2026-97229); Alpine rebuilt the server and reset passwords. Fresh vs Oct 27 security roundup (Node2Shell, PolyShell, Oracle EBS, Tailscale patch).
  • **Quick hits**: NVIDIA RTX Spark laptops from ASUS, Dell, HP, Lenovo, Microsoft, MSI now available from $2,599, plus Nemotron 3.5 models. Altera Agilex 27 FPGA claims 1.8x performance per watt; samples H2 2027. Marvell reportedly in advanced talks to buy Lumentum (Marvell −9.4%, Lumentum +4.3%); Intel and Amazon report after close today.

Full transcript

Alex: Good morning, and welcome to Daily Tech News for Thursday, October twenty-ninth. I am Alex.

Jordan: And I am Jordan. Yesterday was one of those days where the market drew a very clear line between two companies spending on the same thing, and decided only one of them is doing it well.

Alex: That is the story of the day. Microsoft and Alphabet both reported earnings after the close on Wednesday, both are pouring staggering sums into artificial intelligence, and investors reacted in completely opposite directions. Microsoft shares rose about four percent in after-hours trading. Alphabet fell about ten percent.

Jordan: Let us start with the numbers, because they explain the split. Microsoft reported revenue of ninety-two point three billion dollars, up nineteen percent from a year earlier, beating analyst expectations. Adjusted earnings per share came in at four dollars and eighty-nine cents, above the four dollars and seventy-three cents Wall Street expected.

Alex: And the engine was Azure. Microsoft disclosed Azure's quarterly revenue in dollars for the first time, and it was thirty-three point two billion dollars, up forty-eight percent. That beat the forty-four to forty-five percent growth Microsoft itself had guided to back in July.

Jordan: Alphabet, meanwhile, actually grew its cloud business faster. Google Cloud revenue rose one hundred and three percent to thirty point seven billion dollars. More than double Azure's growth rate. And yet the stock fell off a cliff. So what went wrong?

Alex: Cash. That is the whole story. Microsoft spent forty-five point two billion dollars on capital expenditures this quarter and still generated eighteen point six billion dollars in free cash flow, more than double what analysts forecast. Alphabet spent fifty-one point four billion dollars, which was more than its operating cash flow, leaving a free cash flow deficit of about nine point one billion dollars.

Jordan: That is Alphabet's second consecutive quarter of negative free cash flow, and only the second time since it went public in two thousand and four. One analyst put it bluntly: investors are no longer giving every hyperscaler a free pass to spend without limit.

Alex: Microsoft also trimmed its capital expenditure guidance to around one hundred and seventy-five billion dollars, from about one hundred and ninety billion, partly by extending data center useful lives. Alphabet, by contrast, raised money through bonds and stock sales to keep funding its buildout.

Jordan: Both companies said the same thing on their calls: demand for AI computing exceeds available capacity. The difference is that Microsoft is paying for that capacity out of its own cash flow, and Alphabet is not. The Federal Reserve also held rates steady on Wednesday in a ten to two vote.

Alex: And the pain spread: Fortinet fell more than twenty-five percent after its own results, ServiceNow nineteen percent. Intel and Amazon report after the close today, so this story is not finished yet.

Jordan: Speaking of big product days, Apple held its Pro and Tell event in Los Angeles on Wednesday, and it delivered the thing we previewed yesterday: the first touchscreen Mac.

Alex: Yesterday we covered Apple's M six MacBook Pro and iMac announcement. What we did not yet have was the star of the show. Apple unveiled new fourteen-inch and sixteen-inch MacBook Pros with what it calls a PROLED XDR display, a tandem OLED panel with up to two thousand nits of peak brightness, a ten million to one contrast ratio, and a refresh rate up to one hundred and twenty hertz.

Jordan: And yes, you can touch it. The touchscreen MacBook Pro brings the iPhone-style Dynamic Island to the Mac, giving quick access to Spotlight, live activities, and Siri AI. It runs Apple's M five Pro or M five Max chips, not the newer M six, so the premium here buys the screen and the touch experience, not faster processing.

Alex: The fourteen-inch touchscreen model starts at two thousand eight hundred and ninety-nine dollars, and the sixteen-inch at three thousand three hundred and ninety-nine, roughly four hundred dollars more than the equivalent non-touch models, which stay on sale.

Jordan: Apple also refreshed the iPad mini with an OLED display and the A twenty Pro chip, from six hundred and ninety-nine dollars. Pre-orders for everything opened Wednesday, with availability starting Friday, November sixth.

Alex: Apple spent years saying touchscreens on laptops were a bad idea, that your arm would get tired reaching for a vertical screen. What changed is partly the display: a tandem OLED panel bright enough that Apple is pitching touch as a new way for professionals to work, not just a checkbox feature.

Jordan: I am skeptical until I try one, but Apple clearly believes creative professionals will use it for scrubbing timelines and pinching canvases. Reviews land with the November sixth launch.

Alex: Over to security now. A hacker collective calling itself the Department of Peace, working with the transparency nonprofit Distributed Denial of Secrets, published an archive codenamed Icepick containing login credentials for more than forty-one thousand Department of Homeland Security employees and contractors.

Jordan: The leak spans seven Homeland Security component agencies, including Immigration and Customs Enforcement and the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency itself. The exposed data includes email addresses, phone numbers, and organizational details, and the attackers claim the breach was a response to the agency's immigration enforcement operations.

Alex: The breach reportedly originated from an Okta single sign-on system, with the most recent dataset update dated August nineteenth. Okta says its own systems were not compromised, and Homeland Security has not yet commented publicly.

Jordan: Two things matter here. First, the targeting risk: tens of thousands of federal personnel, including undercover agents, now have their contact details and roles in public. Second, the identity angle: if credentials tied to a single sign-on integration can be harvested at this scale, every organization should be asking whether its own identity integrations could be a similar weak point.

Alex: In other security news, the clock runs out today on two critical PHP flaws collectively called PHP two Shell. The Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency added them to its known exploited vulnerabilities catalog on October twenty-sixth, giving federal agencies until today to patch. The flaws allow an unauthenticated attacker to run code on PHP servers, and researchers say they have already seen successful exploit attempts on honeypot servers.

Jordan: And Cisco warned on Wednesday that attackers are actively exploiting a flaw in its Secure Firewall software that can force firewalls to reload, knocking out VPN connectivity. That one is tracked as CVE twenty twenty-six dash seven six four one eight, with a severity score of eight point six out of ten.

Alex: Quick hits before we go. NVIDIA says RTX Spark laptops from partners including ASUS, Dell, HP, Lenovo, Microsoft, and MSI are now available, starting at two thousand five hundred and ninety-nine dollars, alongside new Nemotron three point five AI models for local processing.

Jordan: Chipmaker Altera unveiled its Agilex twenty-seven FPGA, claiming one point eight times the performance per watt of its previous generation, aimed at AI data centers. Samples are not expected until the second half of next year.

Alex: And Marvell Technology fell more than nine percent on reports it is in advanced talks to buy data-center optics maker Lumentum, whose shares rose on the news. Neither company has commented.

Jordan: That is the show for Thursday, October twenty-ninth. The headline takeaway: the AI spending era is entering its prove-it phase. Microsoft proved it can fund the buildout and still print cash. Alphabet has not, at least not this quarter.

Alex: Plus, Apple finally touched the Mac, literally, and a massive federal credential leak reminds us that identity systems are now prime targets. We will be back tomorrow with whatever Intel and Amazon bring after the close today. Thanks for listening.
